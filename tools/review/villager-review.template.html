About these villagers

  • The man wears a knee-length tunic in your colour with a leather belt, leg wrappings and shoes, with a short beard and hair. The woman wears a mid-calf dress in your colour and a linen headscarf.
  • Walking, each foot stays planted while the body moves over it, then rolls onto the toes and swings through with the knee bending. The hips turn a little with each step and the arms swing opposite the legs.
  • The tunic and dress are skinned to the legs, so they swing with each stride instead of the legs poking through. I checked this by casting rays out from every leg point, frame by frame. No leg point shows through at any frame of the walk, in either the full or the light version, and no hand passes into the cloth or the body.
  • Loads are held in front of the chest in both arms: a bundle of logs, a basket of food with meat, a sack of gold ore, or a block of stone.
  • The tools are new too; pick one under “Tool in hand” to see it. They are hung from the right hand, except the basket in the left, and the jobs will move them.
