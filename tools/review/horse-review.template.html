What changed since version 1

  • Each leg bends: at the knee in front, the hock behind, and the fetlock on all four. In the air the front knees fold back and the hind hocks flex; on the ground the leg straightens under the weight.
  • Real gaits: the walk's four beats, the trot's diagonal pairs, and the canter's three beats with a moment in the air. The body rises, dips and rocks over the legs.
  • About three times the detail: shoulders, elbows, stifles, hocks, tendons, fetlocks, chestnuts and ergots, heel bulbs, jaw, cheeks, nostrils and hollow ears. The mane and tail are made of separate locks.
  • Real coat colours: bays and duns have black legs, mane, tail and muzzle, greys are dappled, and duns have a dark stripe down the back. White blazes and socks are optional.

Before you decide

  • It costs 11 times the triangles of today's horse. In the game I'd add a lighter version, around 2,500 triangles, for normal zoom, where the two look the same. I'll measure the frame rate before committing.
  • In the game each unit's speed picks the gait: the canter for scouts, light cavalry and horse archers, the trot for knights. Hooves stay planted while a unit moves in a straight line. They will still slip a little when it turns or stops, as in most strategy games.
  • A foreleg carrying weight can ride up a few centimetres into the shoulder rather than buckling at the knee. Horses have no collarbone, so real shoulders move like this too.
  • Camels keep their current model for now.
